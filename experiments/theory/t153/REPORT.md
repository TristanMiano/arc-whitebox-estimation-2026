## What changed our next action

Most of the round was disappointing for the original nearby routes. More
accurate fourth-core predictors gave small raw gains that could not pay their
cost. Much cheaper matrix closures lost important history even when current
moments were preserved exactly. A new trace-invisible fourth source also failed
in its first integrated realization. None of the completed modified full
estimators improved the adjusted objective on the exploratory pair.

The useful late result came from retaining the native tensor family and changing
**which directions receive its limited rank**. A local coupled metric already
improved the same-rank comparator by about3.75% raw, but still lost to the
incumbent. Weighting the native projection for future response then produced
much larger saved-state improvements. A cheap diagonal-Gaussian forecast kept
the full teacher-metric effect surprisingly well. A fixed128-column sketch
retained part of it, and including intermediate responses improved the known
near-horizon tradeoff.

These are source-response errors at two saved states of **one known network**,
not final estimation scores. The weighted dual projector supplies much of the
benefit; a Euclidean projection onto the same trial span often loses it.
The complete nominee adds combined multi-layer sketches, fresh metric refresh,
a narrow range finder and nested weighted projections. Those pieces and their
end-to-end feedback have not been tested together.

NFC128's provisional steady cost is500–530B FLOPs versus558.47B for the current
matched parent, including substantial metric/projection overhead. The first
actual meter must replace that estimate. My subjective chance of a retained
official win is **15–30%**, with **5–20% adjusted improvement conditional on
success**. The full inventory preserves larger state-change alternatives and
cheaper reserves, with separate probabilities. These are planning judgments,
not statistical confidence from the reused targets.
