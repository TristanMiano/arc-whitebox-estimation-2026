## What the theory contributed

The strongest own result is a **homogeneous fourth-core conversion**. For the
degree-one outputs of this network, a covariance-aligned part of a modeled
fourth cumulant can be represented by existing second- and third-order
responses. The remaining paired fourth term has an additional source
correction that fits the JSP factor with elementwise work. This gives a
concrete HGC reserve without another dense matrix product or history family.
Its static cost is about728–731B FLOPs, conditional on replacing the actual
fourth metric with the public method's average-metric approximation.

The exact identity does **not** prove that the resulting truncated recurrence
is more accurate. Generated composed-function examples are encouraging in
several regimes and adverse in others. A separate exact Gamma/Beta example
shows how an equivalent homogeneous representation can have a worse finite
closure. The public coefficient table would also be transferred to a changed
trajectory. These are the remaining experimental questions, rather than a
requirement for a general convergence theorem before a bounded test.

Other progress narrows the next research choices:

- A gated completion metric makes the discarded-response objective explicit,
  but still targets an idealized modeled tensor whose full retention lost in
  T145. It remains a reserve.
- A finite connected fourth-source program and trace formulas make a fitted
  coefficient replacement more concrete. Noise looks substantially better when
  measured against the combined source rather than a small signed component;
  complete coupling and cost remain unresolved.
- A mixed-query compressor preserves the immediate query very accurately but
  loses later history. More solver accuracy did not fix it. Park that spelling.
- A positive-cone weighted-law state preserves the desired homogeneous
  observables exactly. Its affordable finite approximation is still open.
