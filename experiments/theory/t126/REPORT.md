## What the theory changed

The complete estimator matters more than the accuracy of an isolated moment.
For parent error e and output change v, the exact risk change is
`2 E[e v] + E[v²]`. T125's retained evidence gives an initially favorable
direction on 7/12 networks, with mean derivative −2.7582e-8, but a full-step
energy of 4.1735e-8 produces its observed mean loss. This preserves a useful
covariance lead without choosing a blend coefficient on those development
networks or reopening the unchanged losing version.

The cheaper opportunity is to separate the odd and even parts of the mean
response, alongside the variance response. These are available from existing
physical source moments. On twelve predeclared input-only cache rows, their
coordinate fields retain substantial variation outside ALG029's sixteen
columns: residual norm fractions .469, .577 and .927. This shows that they add
information; it does **not** establish predictive value. An exact transport
identity connects the mean field to T117's retained positive mean-correction
evidence, offering a small route back to that signal.

A stronger limitation emerged for moment-only successors. An explicit
homogeneous ReLU construction matches a standard Gaussian's first four
moments while its ReLU expectation is **2.6064% higher**. A finite-moment
construction extends the obstruction when sufficient architecture width is
allowed. This is an architecture-class counterexample, not a claim that such
distributions are typical of challenge networks. It supports retaining the
direct shape remainder instead of replacing it with a universal fourth-moment
formula.

The conditional second option measures a projected distribution through
radially integrated bin moments and a smooth conditional ReLU response,
removing its Gaussian expectation and Hermite orders one through three.
It detects part of the four-moment counterexample that a fourth-cumulant
correction misses. Synthetic interpolation checks have maximum absolute error
about 1.18e-6 across thirty mixture fixtures; the radial-moment and large-shape
gamma calculations have separate numerical checks. These are mathematical
fixtures, not challenge-score evidence or a production implementation.

The limitations are concrete. A selected direction can be exactly Gaussian
while orthogonal shape still changes each marginal ReLU mean. Conditional
projections also need not commute: a valid Gaussian control before a second
conditioning step can increase variance after it. Thus the shape option is
only an additive, fitted feature; its population identities do not prove a
win for the whole capped estimator.
